Ouroth, the [[Daeherut|Daeheran]] God of the Drowned, Sunken Things and the Sea's Custody, opposition to the [[Nairene]] [[Oius]]. Ouroth is what the sea keeps: the drowned, the sunken, the memories dragged under and held. He is not the sea's cruelty but its custody, coiled around everything it has taken and bound to return nothing until it chooses. The Tideborne say the sea is an aspect of both Oius and Ouroth, and refuse to elaborate.

**Type:** Deity  
**Demanse:** _[[The Drowned Courts]]_  
**Alternative names:**  
_The Coiled Keeper, the Deep Custodian_

**Tenents**:  
_What the sea takes, the sea keeps. The drowned are not lost, only held. Do not ask the deep for what it has not offered._

_The drowned_

## Depictions
A vast serpent whose coils are every wreck ever lost, biting its own tail; sailors dispute whether the circle is a cage or a cradle.

## Holy Symbol
The holy symbol of Ouroth, _A coiled serpent swallowing its tail_, is worn openly by the clergy and marks the doors of the temples. Like all Daeheran symbols it is meant to be read alongside its Nairene counterpart, for each pair of faces makes one truth.

## Worship
Every port of Calenmir drops the Last Cup over the stern for Ouroth's drowned, and his floating Doorless Funerals, wreaths set adrift for bodies the sea kept, open the Ransom Regatta. Salvage-divers pray to him before a dive and give the first coin of every find back to the water.

## Clergy
Clerics of Ouroth conduct the sea's funerals, keep the drowned-names registers of every port, and certify salvage rights by deciding what the sea has offered versus what it has kept. Their most dreaded duty is to declare a wreck "kept", after which no one dives it again, and every wrecker in Calenmir knows better than to test them.

## Practices and Rituals
The Last Cup poured over the stern; the Floating Wreath for the unreturned; the First Coin given back at every salvage.

## Temples
Temples of Ouroth are tidal: shrines that stand in water at high tide and dry at low, with the serpent's circle carved where the tide leaves offerings.

## Artifacts
The Kept Bell, which rings underwater wherever the drowned lie; the Salvage Coin, which grows cold when a wreck is not meant to be raised.
