Mordhal, the [[Daeherut|Daeheran]] God of Final Death and the Rest of the Dead, opposition to the [[Nairene]] [[Axtia]]. The dead are owed their rest, and Mordhal is the one who guards it. He is final death: not the ending of life, which belongs to others, but the ending of endings, the door that closes and stays closed. He is the mortal enemy of necromancy in all its forms.

**Type:** Deity  
**Demanse:** _[[The Barrowfields]]_  
**Alternative names:**  
_The Doorkeeper, the Patient Brother_

**Tenents**:  
_The dead are owed their rest. Nothing walks that he has closed. Mourn, then let go._

## Depictions
A tall, still figure in undyed grey holding an unlit lantern; his face is not frightening, only finished.

## Holy Symbol
The holy symbol of Mordhal, _A scythe crossed with an hourglass, both at rest_, is worn openly by the clergy and marks the doors of the temples. Like all Daeheran symbols it is meant to be read alongside its Nairene counterpart, for each pair of faces makes one truth.

## Worship
Every funeral in the Duniin Eliph lands ends with the Knock: the officiant raps three times on the coffin lid or the grave-cover and says "the door is shut", in Mordhal's name. Families of the missing hold his Doorless Rite, a funeral with an empty coffin, so the lost are not left standing.

## Clergy
Clerics of Mordhal are the Grave clergy of Calenmir, the funeral-keepers, and the front line against the undead. They are respected everywhere and loved nowhere, and they know it. Their great vow: nothing I bury walks again, and everything that walks wrongly, I put down.

## Practices and Rituals
The Knock at every funeral; the Doorless Rite for the lost and the drowned; the Watch, a night spent armed beside a fresh grave when necromancy is feared.

## Temples
Temples of Mordhal stand at the edge of graveyards, never inside them: a hall with one door, one lantern, and a bench for the mourners. The largest is said to hold the registry of every Knock performed in Calenmir.

## Artifacts
The Shutting Lantern, whose light makes undead flinch from any grave it is set upon; the Patient Scythe, which blunts itself if drawn in anger against the living.
