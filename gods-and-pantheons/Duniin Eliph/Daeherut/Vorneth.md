Vorneth, the [[Daeherut|Daeheran]] God of Fallow Fields, Rationing and Famine, opposition to the [[Nairene]] [[Sirus]]. Vorneth is the withheld bounty: the fallow year, the rationed store, the fast. He is hunger's answer, not hunger's cause, teaching that the earth must rest and so must those who live from it, and that "enough" is a holy word.

## Depictions
A gaunt farmer with a full granary at his back and an open hand offered forward; his belt is always drawn one notch tighter than it needs to be.

## Holy Symbol
The holy symbol of Vorneth, _A withered stalk of grain beside a full one_, is worn openly by the clergy and marks the doors of the temples. Like all Daeheran symbols it is meant to be read alongside its Nairene counterpart, for each pair of faces makes one truth.

## Worship
Farmers leave Vorneth's Sheaf standing at the field's edge at harvest, so the field may keep something of itself. In famine years his Ration Rite, where the granary-master serves themselves last, is the difference between a town and a riot, and everyone knows it.

## Clergy
Clerics of Vorneth are the keepers of granaries, the treasurers of temples, and the ones communities turn to when the stores run low. Their vows bind them to eat last and to keep stores honest, and their audits are feared more than any tax collector's.

## Practices and Rituals
Vorneth's Sheaf left at the field's edge at harvest; the Fallow Blessing, no field worked more than two years in three without his formal release; the Ration Rite in famine.

## Temples
Temples of Vorneth are granaries with a shrine at the door, and his holiest places are simply well-kept stores.

## Artifacts
The Honest Bushel, which always shows the true measure; the Fallow Bell, rung to open and close a rationing.
