Talveth, the [[Daeherut|Daeheran]] God of Orderly Endings, Demolition and Salvage, opposition to the [[Nairene]] [[Dhorer]]. Talveth is the orderly end of things: not the Void's unwriting of history, but the craftsmanlike taking-apart, the demolition that yields material, the entropy that returns a finished thing to its parts so that parts may become new things. He is the forge's brother, not its foe: Dhorer makes, Talveth clears the bench.

## Depictions
A methodical workman with a smith's build, carrying a small hammer and a small crowbar, standing in a demolished room swept perfectly clean behind him.

## Holy Symbol
The holy symbol of Talveth, _A cracked anvil whose crack reveals fresh ore_, is worn openly by the clergy and marks the doors of the temples. Like all Daeheran symbols it is meant to be read alongside its Nairene counterpart, for each pair of faces makes one truth.

## Worship
Talveth receives the clearances: the builder prays to him before demolition, the heir before finally selling the hoard, the community before a ruin is taken down to build again. His followers' oath against hoarding makes them the natural enemies of misers and dragons both, and the Wyrmlords' hoard-cults consider him the most dangerous god in Calenmir.

## Clergy
Clerics of Talveth are the demolition-keepers and salvage-assessors of Calenmir, and among the most trusted: they decide what can be unmade, salvage it honestly, and famously take no bribes, which everyone finds unsettling. They also serve as the practical auxiliaries of the Balance against the Void, for what Talveth has taken apart to its threads, the Void cannot unwrite.

## Practices and Rituals
The Clearing, a formal demolition blessed and salvaged; the Breaking of the Hoard, where an over-kept thing is finally released; the Swept Floor, the ritual that ends the rite.

## Temples
Temples of Talveth are clear ground: swept courts beside a small workshop where the salvaged is sorted and sold, proceeds to the poor.

## Artifacts
The Parting Hammer, which unmakes to parts without damage; the Hoard-Measure, which shows what any hoard is truly worth, to the horror of hoarders.
