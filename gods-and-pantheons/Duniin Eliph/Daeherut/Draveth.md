Draveth, the [[Daeherut|Daeheran]] God of the Trembling Earth, Erosion and the Buried, opposition to the [[Nairene]] [[Vaone]]. Vaone is the mountain's majesty: the standing peak, the stable stone. Draveth is the mountain's other truth: the tremor, the slide, the patient grinding of stone to sand, and the deep holding of everything the earth has taken in. He is not destruction but change at the land's own pace, and every cliff, every scree-slope, every fossil bed is his slow work.

## Depictions
A vast, slow figure made of scree and shifting gravel, half-landslide and half-old man, whose face is a cliff profile seen from far below; some hold that the Draveth of legend is the summit's collapse, and that he is the only god reliably visible from a distance.

## Holy Symbol
The holy symbol of Draveth, _a mountain with a cracked base_, is worn openly by the clergy and marks the doors of the temples. Like all Daeheran symbols it is meant to be read alongside its Nairene counterpart, for each pair of faces makes one truth.

## Worship
Miners, quarrymen and stonemasons pray to Draveth before the deep places, and every dwarven hold of Calenmir keeps his Tremble-Rite before new workings: a moment of stillness in which the hold listens for the mountain's answer. Hill-farmers terrace and build light in his name, and what a landslide takes is never rebuilt on, for that ground is now his.

## Clergy
Clerics of Draveth are the pit-and-mine clergy of the world: they sound the deep places before workings, warn of the tremor, and seal workings the mountain refuses, a practice that has made them the steady enemies of greedy thanes since the first dig. They keep the registries of the buried, for the earth is Calenmir's oldest tomb, and conduct the rites for miners lost below, whose bodies the mountain keeps, which is held to be a holding and not a loss.

## Practices and Rituals
The Tremble-Rite of stillness before entering the deep places; the Sealing of workings the mountain refuses; the Listening, held after every quake to learn what the ground meant; the Fossil Walk, in which the ancient dead of the stone are honoured as the earth's memory.

## Temples
Temples of Draveth are always partly underground and never entirely stable on purpose: low halls of fitted scree at the foot of slides, where the ceiling is allowed to settle. The oldest temple is a fault-line wall carved with every name the ground has taken.

## Artifacts
The Sounding Stone, which hums a day before a quake; the Patient Spade, which turns scree to soil in a season and soil to garden in a year.
