Vharnos, the [[Daeherut|Daeheran]] God of the Rout, the Red Field and the Truce, opposition to the [[Nairene]] [[Qhadar]]. Qhadar is war's honour: the charge, the duel, the glorious death. Vharnos is everything after: the broken run, the field of both sides' dead, the weary mercy of a war that has outlived its cause. He is not war's cruelty but war's ending, and no war in Calenmir is considered truly over until he has been honoured.

## Depictions
A gaunt, armoured figure walking a red field with visor open and weapons sheathed, followed by patient white ravens; his armour is dented everywhere and patched nowhere, for he has no more battles to win.

## Holy Symbol
The holy symbol of Vharnos, _a broken spear beneath a white raven_, is worn openly by the clergy and marks the doors of the temples. Like all Daeheran symbols it is meant to be read alongside its Nairene counterpart, for each pair of faces makes one truth.

## Worship
Vharnos is worshipped by every soldier who wants a war to end, and by every farmer whose field was a battlefield. The Crows Eat First rite, the first meat of the victory feast thrown to the field birds, is his; skipping it is held to invite the next war. His Truce of the White Raven, a white banner flown to pause a battle for the burial and rescue of the fallen, is honoured even by armies that respect nothing else, and breaking it is the one act that makes Korvath's and Qhadar's worshippers stand on the same side.

## Clergy
Clerics of Vharnos are the battlefield harvesters: after every engagement they walk the field under the white raven, aid the wounded of both sides, and bury the dead of both sides in one row. They keep the Red Field registries of the fallen, count the crows, for a lean crow-year means a bad truce somewhere, and are the mediators of ceasefires: a truce sworn on Vharnos' broken spear has never been broken twice by the same commander.

## Practices and Rituals
The Walking of the Red Field after every battle; the Truce of the White Raven; the Crows Eat First rite at victory feasts; the Laying Down, where a veteran formally ends their war before the broken spear and is freed from their oaths of vengeance.

## Temples
Temples of Vharnos are burial-fields kept as gardens, with the broken spear planted at the centre and both sides' names cut into the same wall. The greatest stands at a pass fought over by three kingdoms and now fought over by none.

## Artifacts
The White Raven Banner, under which no truce may be broken; the Marshal's Ledger, in which every oath of vengeance formally laid down is blotted out for good.
