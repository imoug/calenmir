Murneth, the [[Daeherut|Daeheran]] Goddess of Grief, Heartbreak and the Ended Song, opposition to the [[Nairene]] [[Ephion]]. Murneth is the other side of every song: the dirge, the elegy, the art that is made because something is gone. She is not the death of passion but its grief-work, and she teaches that the ended song is not silence, it is what the next song is built from.

## Depictions
A veiled mourner holding a broken lyre from which one string still sings; the tear on her cheek is always the most recently painted in her images.

## Holy Symbol
The holy symbol of Murneth, _A broken lyre with one unbroken string_, is worn openly by the clergy and marks the doors of the temples. Like all Daeheran symbols it is meant to be read alongside its Nairene counterpart, for each pair of faces makes one truth.

## Worship
Mourners commission Murneth's elegies rather than simple headstones: a dirge is her proper memorial, and Calenmir's great funerals are judged by their music. The widowed and the bereaved keep her Unstringing, laying down a loved one's instrument for a year before it is played again.

## Clergy
Clerics of Murneth are the funeral-singers, the elegists, and the keepers of the customs that let the bereaved feel at their own pace. They train alongside Mordhal's clergy, who close the door, and open the singing afterwards; the two orders share every graveyard in Calenmir without a single recorded quarrel.

## Practices and Rituals
The Elegy commissioned in place of a monument; the Unstringing of a beloved instrument for a year; the Stillnight Requiem, sung in every hall that lost someone the year before.

## Temples
Temples of Murneth are concert halls of mourning, quiet and famous for their acoustics: the great ones hold one note in the air for the length of a slow breath after the choir stops.

## Artifacts
The Unbroken String, an instrument strung with which cannot sound false in a dirge; the Widowed Lyre, whose one note eases grief to hear.
