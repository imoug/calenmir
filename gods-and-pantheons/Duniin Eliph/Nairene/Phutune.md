Phutune, the [[Nairene|Nairean]] Goddess of Nature and Forests in opposition to [[Daeherut]] [[Karneth]]. Phutune is a nurturing deity, connected to the beauty and vitality of the natural world. She is depicted surrounded by lush foliage and woodland creatures. Druids and nature enthusiasts revere her for harmony with the environment.

## Depictions
An elder wood elven woman, commonly garbed in green practical clothes with a platinum diadem topped with a shining emerald. With her she carries a gnarled beech staff.

## Holy Symbol
Phutune's holy symbol, a summer oak tree, beautifully captures her essence as the goddess of nature and forests.
- The summer oak tree symbolizes strength, growth, and the deep connection between nature and life. It represents Phutune's role in nurturing the natural world, fostering balance, and ensuring the flourishing of ecosystems.
- This symbol serves as a reminder of Phutune's importance in preserving the beauty and vitality of the forests, and her influence on the cycles of nature in the world of Calenmir. It embodies the reverence for the natural world and the harmony that she instills among her followers.

## Further Lore
Phutune, Goddess of Nature and Forests, a verdant guardian of the wild.
### Practices and Rituals
- Offerings to the Forest: Followers often leave offerings of fruit, nuts, or flowers in groves and sacred places within the forest to honor Phutune.
- Tree Planting Ceremonies: Planting new trees is seen as a sacred act, symbolizing growth, renewal, and respect for nature.
- Animal Blessings: Animals are often brought to sacred groves to receive blessings from Phutune, ensuring their health and fertility.
- Seasonal Festivals: Festivals celebrate the changing seasons, recognizing the cyclical nature of life and the interconnectedness of all living things.
### Dogma and Beliefs
- The Sacredness of Nature: Nature is a divine manifestation, and all living things are considered sacred.
- The Interconnectedness of Life: All plants, animals, and natural elements are interconnected and interdependent.
- The Balance of Nature: Maintaining balance in nature is crucial, and followers are encouraged to live in harmony with the wilderness.
- The Power of the Forest: The forest is a place of magic, mystery, and spiritual renewal.
### Clerics of Phutune
Clerics of Phutune are often druids, rangers, or herbalists. They are skilled in communicating with animals, healing with plants, and protecting the wilderness.
### Temples of Phutune
Temples of Phutune are often located in secluded groves or deep within forests. They may be natural clearings or simple structures built from natural materials.
### Artifacts of Phutune
- The Staff of the Wildwood: A magical staff that grants its wielder control over plants and animals.
- The Acorn of Life: A magical acorn that can grow into a powerful and ancient tree.
- The Cloak of Leaves: A magical cloak that allows the wearer to blend seamlessly into the forest.
