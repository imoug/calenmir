Elven name meaning "the balance" or more literally "two faces or aspects" of nature, sometimes shortened to Duniin. The term describes two opposing pantheons of deities which represent the natural world: the [[Nairene]], or the green touched, representing life, light and growth, and the [[Daeherut]], the shadow lords, representing death, darkness and decay.

## The Shadow Lords
The Daeherut are not evil gods. They are the world's honest other half: rest, rot, winter, the clean kill, the fallow field, the ended song, the shut door. No harvest comes without the fallow year; no forest grows without the clearing fire; no grief heals that is never felt. To worship the Daeherut alone is imbalance, but to ignore them is worse, and every settled land of Calenmir keeps some observance of both faces.

The Nairene are served gladly and the Daeherut dutifully, and the saying goes that a farmer prays to [[Sirus]] for the harvest and to [[Vorneth]] for the field's rest, and means both.

## Worship
- Clergy of the Daeherut are respected and kept at arm's length: funeral-keepers, plague-keepers, granary-keepers, asylum-keepers. They are the custodians of what life prefers not to look at, and settled communities do not function without them.
- Household observance is dual: [[Vorneth]]'s sheaf at the field's edge, [[Mordhal]]'s Knock at the funeral, the whisper to the dark, [[Selvane]]'s Quiet Hour. Ordinary piety keeps both pantheons' small rites.
- Only the [[Void]] is anathema to both faces alike. What the Daeherut unmake, they unmake to threads that can be rewoven; what the Void unwrites, never was. Void-cults are held by both pantheons to be outside the Balance.
